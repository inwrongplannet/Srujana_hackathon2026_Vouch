# Vouch — Three Pain Points with Statistical Evidence

## 1. No credit history does not necessarily mean poor creditworthiness
**Number:** In a TransUnion CIBIL–CSC programme covering **150,000 consumers**, **19% were not eligible for a numerical CIBIL Score**, potentially because they had no credit history.

**Why it matters for Vouch:** A missing bureau score is not, by itself, proof that someone cannot repay. Vouch proposes adding verified financial evidence and accountable endorsements as supplementary signals.

**Caveat:** This was a specific programme population, not a nationally representative estimate; it does not establish that all 19% were creditworthy.

Source: https://newsroom.transunion.in/transunion-cibil-and-csc-partnership-benefits-15-lakh-consumers-in-first-year/

## 2. Unexpected financial setbacks can create real financial pressure
**Number:** Out-of-pocket payments accounted for **43.4% of India's total health expenditure in 2022–23**—approximately **₹3.83 lakh crore** in household spending, according to the Ministry of Health and Family Welfare's National Health Accounts.

**Why it matters for Vouch:** A major expense can change short-term cash flow. An assessment should examine current income, obligations, and affordability rather than infer the full story from a score movement alone.

**Caveat:** This is health-spending data. It does not measure how often medical emergencies cause credit-score declines or loan defaults.

Source: https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2265816&lang=1&reg=3

## 3. Borrowers may face credit rejection without understanding why
**Numbers:** A ZET study of **more than 1,000 respondents across India** reported that **45% had never checked their credit score** and **about one-third said they had been rejected for a loan or credit card due to a poor or absent credit history, often without an explanation**.

**Why it matters for Vouch:** This supports the need for clearer, personalized explanations of an assessment—what evidence affected it, what remains uncertain, and what the applicant can do next.

**Caveat:** These are survey findings, not official nationwide population estimates. They reflect respondents' reported experiences and do not prove that credit bureaus themselves provide no explanations. Vouch should be framed as offering a transparent explanation of its own assessment, not as a claim that CIBIL offers none.

## Pitch-ready summary

- **19% of 150,000 consumers** in one CIBIL–CSC programme were not eligible for a numerical score, potentially due to missing credit history.
- **43.4% of India's health expenditure** in 2022–23 was paid out of pocket by households.
- In a survey of **1,000+ people**, **45% had never checked their score**, while **about one-third reported rejection linked to poor or absent credit history, often without an explanation**.

**Important:** These figures establish context for the problems; they do not prove that Vouch improves lending outcomes. Claims about reduced defaults or better approval rates require independent validation.
