# Vouch — Business Model
**Business model definition | India | 25 October 2026**

## 1. Executive summary

Vouch is a **credit-assessment framework for lending institutions**. It is designed to let banks, NBFCs, fintech lenders, and other suitable credit providers consider additional, permissioned borrower information—including verified financial evidence and accountable peer endorsements—alongside their existing assessment processes.

Vouch is a technology provider, **not a lender**. It complements existing credit assessment; it does not claim to replace CIBIL, conventional credit scores, or a lender’s underwriting policy. The lender retains responsibility for the final lending decision.

Vouch will commercialise the framework through two deployment models:

1. **Institution-owned deployment:** a lending institution licenses and integrates Vouch into its own systems.
2. **Independent Vouch platform:** multiple institutions access Vouch through a shared platform or API.

The core business model is limited to credit assessment. Financial-readiness tracking, borrower education, credit-improvement features, and other consumer-facing expansions are future scope and are excluded from the current revenue model.

---

## 2. The problem Vouch addresses

Conventional credit assessment can provide an incomplete picture for some applicants, particularly people with limited formal credit histories or financial circumstances that are not fully reflected in existing credit records.

Vouch explores whether additional, appropriately verified evidence can help lenders assess these applicants more comprehensively. The framework may combine:

- **Existing credit information:** the lender’s established credit data and assessment process.
- **Verified financial evidence:** relevant information available through appropriate, lawful, consent-based processes.
- **Accountable peer endorsements:** signals from people in a borrower’s network, designed with controls for quality, independence, fraud, and collusion.

The purpose is to provide a **complementary assessment layer**, not to treat social connections as proof of repayment ability. Vouch’s incremental value must be measured against the lender’s existing process.

## 3. Value proposition

### For lending institutions
- An additional assessment capability that can fit into existing lending workflows.
- A potential way to examine applicants whose conventional credit histories provide limited information.
- Deployment options ranging from institution-controlled installation to a shared platform/API.
- A commercial model that can combine implementation revenue with recurring licence, subscription, and usage revenue.

### For borrowers
- The opportunity for relevant, verified information beyond a conventional credit file to be considered, where a lender chooses to use the framework.
- No guarantee of loan approval, a better credit score, a lower interest rate, or improved repayment outcomes.

### For Vouch
- Business-to-business revenue from institutional customers.
- Recurring revenue opportunities through licences or subscriptions.
- Usage-based revenue as assessment volumes grow.
- The possibility of serving multiple institutions using common platform capabilities.

---

## 4. Customer segments and buyers

**Primary customers:** organisations that originate or assess credit.

- Banks
- Non-Banking Financial Companies (NBFCs)
- Fintech lending platforms
- Microfinance institutions, where the product fits their underwriting process

**Economic buyer:** typically the institution’s business, credit-risk, technology, or procurement leadership, depending on the deployment.

**Operational stakeholders:** underwriting teams, product managers, engineering/integration teams, information security, privacy/compliance, and vendor-risk management.

Vouch should initially prioritise institutions with a clearly defined assessment problem, a feasible data-access route, and the ability to run a controlled pilot.

## 5. Commercial model A — Institution-owned deployment

### Description
A lending institution adopts Vouch and deploys it within its own technology environment or through an agreed hybrid arrangement.

### Operating flow
1. Vouch contracts with the institution.
2. Both parties define scope, permitted data, security, integration, and evaluation criteria.
3. Vouch provides the framework and implementation support.
4. The institution integrates it into the relevant assessment workflow.
5. The institution uses the resulting assessment information within its own underwriting policy and retains control of lending decisions.
6. Vouch receives the agreed implementation and recurring fees.

### Revenue streams
- **Implementation fee:** one-time payment for deployment and integration.
- **Annual licence:** recurring payment for use of the framework.
- **Assessment/usage fee:** optional charge tied to measured volume.
- **Maintenance and support:** included in the licence initially or contracted separately.
- **Enterprise services:** customisation, dedicated deployment, or additional service levels.

### Illustrative starting price card
These are planning assumptions for financial modelling, not established market prices or customer commitments.

| Component | Initial modelling assumption |
|---|---:|
| Implementation fee | ₹3,00,000 per institution |
| Annual licence | ₹4,80,000 per institution |
| Usage fee | ₹10 per assessment |
| Maintenance/support | Included initially; may be separately priced in enterprise contracts |

### Advantages
- Potentially larger individual contracts.
- Greater institutional control over deployment and data flows.
- A clear route for lenders that require dedicated or hybrid infrastructure.
- Recurring licence revenue once deployed.

### Challenges
- Institution-specific integrations can increase delivery time and cost.
- Procurement, security review, and vendor approval may lengthen sales cycles.
- Revenue may be concentrated among a small number of customers.
- Customisation can limit repeatability if not carefully scoped.

## 6. Commercial model B — Independent Vouch platform

### Description
Vouch operates a centrally managed platform or API that multiple lending institutions can use under separate contracts.

### Operating flow
1. A lender signs up and completes commercial, security, and compliance onboarding.
2. Vouch integrates with the lender’s workflow through an agreed interface.
3. The lender submits permitted assessment requests.
4. Vouch returns assessment outputs under the agreed service and data-handling terms.
5. The lender makes the final credit decision.
6. The lender pays a subscription and/or usage fees.

### Revenue streams
- **Onboarding/integration fee:** one-time charge for connecting a lender.
- **Monthly or annual subscription:** recurring platform-access fee.
- **Per-assessment fee:** usage-based revenue.
- **Enterprise contract:** negotiated pricing for larger volumes, custom integrations, or service-level commitments.

### Illustrative starting price card
These are planning assumptions, not verified willingness-to-pay figures.

| Component | Initial modelling assumption |
|---|---:|
| Onboarding fee | ₹1,50,000 per lender |
| Platform subscription | ₹30,000 per month per lender |
| Usage fee | ₹10 per assessment |
| Enterprise pricing | Negotiated based on scope, volume, and service levels |

### Advantages
- Common infrastructure can serve multiple customers.
- Subscription revenue can provide a recurring baseline.
- Usage revenue can increase as assessment volumes grow.
- A standard product can become more repeatable as integrations mature.

### Challenges
- Vouch bears more responsibility for operating and securing the shared service.
- It must manage multi-client access, service reliability, data segregation, and support.
- Early growth may require investment before recurring revenue covers fixed costs.
- Customer acquisition and retention become central to the economics.

## 7. Pricing strategy and stages

Pricing should be structured around deployment effort, ongoing service cost, usage, and demonstrated value to the lender.

### Stage 1 — Paid pilot
- Fixed scope and duration.
- Defined assessment-volume cap.
- Agreed integration and support boundaries.
- Written success criteria and evaluation plan.
- Pilot pricing designed to cover direct delivery costs where feasible.

### Stage 2 — Initial production deployment
- Institution-owned model: implementation fee plus annual licence.
- Independent platform: onboarding fee plus monthly subscription.
- Set clear limits for included support, usage, and custom work.

### Stage 3 — Usage-based pricing
- Introduce or refine per-assessment fees after actual delivery costs are measured.
- Use transparent volume bands.
- Offer discounts only where committed volume or lower unit cost supports them.

### Stage 4 — Enterprise pricing
- Negotiate annual commitments, integration scope, service levels, support, and security obligations.
- Price custom work separately where it creates material additional cost.
- Review renewal pricing against usage, delivery costs, and demonstrated value.

### Pricing leverage
Vouch’s sustainable pricing leverage should come from:
1. Measurable value to the lender.
2. Reliable and secure delivery.
3. Integration quality and lower operational friction.
4. Repeatable implementation and support.
5. Volume commitments and predictable usage.

Vouch should not assume it can charge a premium until it demonstrates value beyond the lender’s current assessment process.

## 8. Cost structure

### Fixed and semi-fixed costs
- Product engineering and maintenance
- Security architecture, testing, and monitoring
- Legal, privacy, compliance, and vendor-risk work
- Core cloud infrastructure and tooling
- Sales, partnerships, and institutional procurement
- Customer support and operations

### Variable and customer-specific costs
- Per-assessment compute and data/API charges
- Identity or evidence verification, where applicable
- Institution-specific integration and implementation effort
- Customer-specific hosting or deployment
- Additional support, reporting, or service-level requirements

The financial model should separate one-time development and implementation costs from recurring operating costs and per-assessment delivery costs.

## 9. Unit economics and financial formulas

### Institution-owned deployment
**Annual revenue**  
= New implementation fees + Annual licence revenue + Assessment revenue + Separately contracted services

**Assessment revenue**  
= Completed paid assessments × Fee per assessment

**Contribution before fixed operating costs**  
= Total revenue − Variable delivery costs − Direct customer-specific delivery costs

### Independent platform
**Annual revenue**  
= New onboarding fees + Subscription revenue + Assessment revenue + Enterprise service revenue

**Subscription revenue**  
= Average active paying lenders × Monthly subscription × 12

**Assessment revenue**  
= Completed paid assessments × Fee per assessment

**Contribution before fixed operating costs**  
= Total revenue − Variable delivery costs − Direct customer-specific delivery costs

### Break-even
Break-even occurs when total contribution covers fixed operating costs. The exact lender count and assessment volume depend on subscription pricing, usage, retention, variable costs, implementation effort, and support burden.

## 10. Go-to-market and rollout

### Phase 1 — Discovery
- Interview target lenders and map the assessment workflow.
- Identify the buyer, budget owner, procurement process, and security requirements.
- Confirm the lawful data-access path and institutional responsibilities.
- Test the proposed pricing with prospective customers.

### Phase 2 — Controlled pilot
- Select one suitable lending partner.
- Agree on scope, permitted data, integration, costs, and success criteria.
- Measure operational performance and cost per assessment.
- Where suitable outcome data is available, evaluate whether Vouch adds incremental predictive value beyond the existing process.

### Phase 3 — Commercial conversion
- Convert a successful pilot into a recurring contract.
- Standardise implementation and support.
- Confirm gross margin and the real cost to serve each customer.
- Select institution-owned or shared-platform delivery based on the customer’s needs.

### Phase 4 — Expansion
- Add customers using repeatable contracts and integration patterns.
- Introduce pricing tiers based on volume and service requirements.
- Monitor retention, implementation time, customer acquisition cost, and contribution margin.

## 13. Regulatory and risk considerations

Vouch’s exact obligations depend on its contractual role, data flows, and deployment structure. Before commercial launch, the model should be reviewed against applicable Indian rules, including the RBI’s Digital Lending Directions, 2025, where relevant.

Key design considerations:
- Establish whether Vouch acts as a technology vendor, a Lending Service Provider, or in another capacity under each arrangement.
- For covered digital-lending arrangements, do not assume that Vouch can charge borrowers directly for services where applicable rules require LSP fees to be paid by the regulated entity.
- Use appropriate consent, purpose limitation, data minimisation, security, access controls, retention rules, and audit trails.
- Define responsibilities for data quality, errors, complaints, incidents, and model oversight.
- Test endorsements for collusion, fraud, bias, and weak relevance to repayment.
- Keep the lender responsible for the final lending decision under the proposed framework.

Official starting point: Reserve Bank of India — https://www.rbi.org.in/

## 14. Key performance indicators

Vouch should monitor commercial, operational, and assessment-quality indicators.

**Commercial**
- Number of contracted and active institutions
- Recurring revenue and revenue per institution
- Pilot-to-contract conversion rate
- Renewal and retention rate
- Customer acquisition cost and payback period

**Operational**
- Time and cost to integrate each institution
- Cost per completed assessment
- Platform availability and response time
- Support burden per customer
- Gross margin and contribution margin

**Assessment evaluation**
- Incremental predictive value compared with the existing process
- False-rejection and false-acceptance rates, where measurable
- Calibration and stability across relevant groups
- Fraud, collusion, and data-quality indicators
- Outcomes from appropriately designed pilots or retrospective evaluations

Vouch should not claim reduced defaults, higher approval rates, or improved credit outcomes until credible evaluation supports those claims.

## 15. Final business-model decision

Vouch will retain two commercial routes:

- **Model A — Institution-owned:** best suited to institutions that require dedicated control, custom integration, or institution-specific deployment.
- **Model B — Independent platform:** best suited to repeatable service delivery across multiple lenders through a shared platform or API.

Both models monetise the same core credit-assessment framework. The choice of deployment is based on customer requirements, integration costs, data governance, recurring economics, and scalability.

**Core business model:** Vouch sells credit-assessment infrastructure to lending institutions through implementation and recurring access fees, with optional usage-based and enterprise revenue.

**Future scope only:** financial-readiness tools, borrower education, credit-improvement features, and other adjacent services are excluded from the current business model and its financial projections.
